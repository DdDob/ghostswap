The problem

Your trade is visible before it's yours.

Public mempools mean anyone can read a pending swap and act on it first. This isn't a bug in any one DEX — it's a property of how public chains order transactions.

Normal swap

Token pair, size, and wallet address are all readable the moment you submit — before the trade even lands. Searchers front-run or sandwich it.

GhostSwap request

Only a commitment hash and an encrypted blob hit the chain. Nothing about the trade — amount, direction, tokens — can be extracted from it.

How it works

Two transactions. One privacy layer.

GhostSwap doesn't fork or wrap Uniswap. It sits in front of it, and only calls its existing router interface — same as any other integrator.

1

Request — encrypted, on-chain

You submit your swap request as an encrypted payload plus a commitment hash. This transaction confirms on-chain immediately, but reveals nothing about the trade itself.

2

Settle — the trade actually executes

A relayer submits the decrypted parameters plus an attestation. The contract verifies it, then calls Uniswap's router directly. This is the moment the trade becomes public — after it's already locked in.
